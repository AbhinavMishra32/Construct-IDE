# Construct 0.8.4

The big one. Mastery stops being a number Construct wrote down and becomes a
conclusion drawn from what you actually did — with the evidence kept, dated,
and readable by you. Plus the update surfaces, rebuilt to stop shouting.

## Mastery is a conclusion now

Construct has always recorded a mastery level for every concept it teaches you,
and it has never recorded anything underneath it. The level was its own reading
of the conversation, with no record of what you had been asked to do, when, or
how it went. Level 4 earned in March read exactly like Level 4 earned this
morning, and a level nobody had ever tested read exactly like one that had
been. The mentor built on all three identically.

There is now an evidence log. Every time you do something with an idea it
writes a row: what you did — answered a question, wrote code, debugged
something, explained it back, or were simply shown it — and, separately, what
that asked of you. Recall, recognise, produce, debug and transfer are five
different abilities. Recognising a closure when you see one is not writing a
closure from nothing, and a record that collapses those two is how a system
convinces itself you are ready for something you are not.

The log is append-only and it is keyed to the concept rather than the project.
Understanding is yours: if you wrote a closure while building a renderer, you
have written a closure, and opening a different project does not unwrite it.

## Readings go stale

A level you earned six months ago is not a level you have today, and pretending
otherwise is the quiet way a curriculum drifts out from under someone.

Every concept now carries a freshness — fresh, fading, cold, or untested —
computed from how long ago its evidence is and how solid that evidence was. The
half-life grows with the level, and it grows with **spacing**: what counts is
the number of separate days you have evidence on, not the number of events, so
four encounters in one sitting cannot buy what four across four days does.
Cramming cannot fake retention here.

Concepts that have gone cold are named to the mentor each turn, with an
instruction to work them back into what you are doing — a question that needs
one, a task that uses it again — rather than reintroducing them from zero as
though you had never met.

None of this is stored. Staleness is a fact about the clock, and a stored copy
of it would be wrong the day after it was written.

## Level 3 is no longer a gate

The rule used to be that a task could only be set once every concept it needed
was at Level 3. Nothing in the application ever enforced it, and as a rule it
was wrong anyway.

What has to be true before you are set work is **coverage**: every idea the work
requires has been taught in this project. The level then decides how much of
the work the task hands you. At Level 1 or 2 you get the shape, the moves named,
and one real decision left to you. At Level 3 you get the gap and the criteria.
At Level 4 and above you get the goal and the mentor stays out of the way.
Scaffolding is a dial, not a door.

## Verdicts that are honest about time

Submitting a task files its evidence immediately, because you did produce
something — but with no verdict on it, because nobody has read it yet. The
verdict lands separately when the mentor has actually gone through your files
against the criteria, and settles every waiting row at once.

The log therefore never claims to have known something before it did.

## Four tools the mentor was asking for and did not have

Construct's prompt had been calling for around twenty tools that do not exist in
this version — a permission firewall, a separate exercise tool, a subtask
reviewer, a file opener. A model told to call something that is not there does
not stop; it improvises around the gap. The prompt now names only real tools,
and four real ones were added to close the gaps that mattered.

**Reading notes back.** The mentor could write a concept note and then never
read it again. It can now fetch any note it wrote, along with the evidence
underneath its level, before building on it or judging work against it.

**Finishing a step.** Path steps could be planned and never completed. The
mentor can now retire a step when you have genuinely met its exit criteria, and
the path moves on.

**Tasks belong to a step.** Practice tasks now carry the path node they were set
for, so the timeline can show what each stage of your project actually asked.

**Learning about you.** Your global profile was written once during the intake
and frozen. The mentor can now add to it from what it sees — how you take an
explanation, what you turn out to already know, what reliably trips you up —
alongside your own answers, which remain yours and are never edited.

The mentor is also shown what you have met in your **other** projects. It still
has to introduce an idea here before teaching from it, but it can now pitch that
introduction at the level you already have instead of starting you from nothing
for the third time.

## You can see all of it

Hovering a step in the sidebar timeline shows what it teaches — each concept
with its level and its freshness — what done looks like, and every task it set
with the verdict.

Opening a concept shows **What this is based on**: the log itself, in plain
words. *Wrote code from scratch · held · 2 days ago.* And when there is nothing
behind a level it says so outright, rather than letting a number imply a
confidence nobody earned.

## The update surfaces

The window you are most likely reading this in.

It used to open with a sparkle in a black tile, a blurred glow behind the
header, a gradient hairline, and the words "A new round for Construct". After
an update it was a tick in a green disc under "Construct just got better". All
of that was the application being pleased with itself in front of someone who
was doing something else.

An update is an interruption. The only honest way to design one is to make it
small, say what it is, and get out of the way. So the version is the title,
because the version is the news. One line of meta under it. The notes as they
were written, fading rather than cut through the middle where they run past the
box. Two buttons.

The corner notification lost its icon entirely — that panel is already inside
Construct, and an app telling you which app it is is something only an installer
needs to do. Its progress bar moved to the bottom edge, where it reads as the
card filling rather than as a lid on it, and it now runs during install too, so
"Installing" no longer sits above an empty bar looking stalled.

## Under the hood

- The evidence log, its indexes, and the path node on a task are new tables and
  columns; your existing projects migrate on first launch.
- The prompt's containment test now pins every replaced line, so the teaching
  behaviour cannot drift by accident.
- 359 tests.
